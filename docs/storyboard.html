Before the headset: the pitch

Most of us have a video we can't step back into. Rewind lets you stand inside it.

Party mode takes an old video and builds the room around it, so you watch the original video exactly as it was filmed while you look around the place it was filmed. Concert mode takes fans' clips of the same song from the internet, finds where each fan stood, and lets you move between their seats with the music in sync.

Everything around the video is generated by AI, not recorded. You can look around, but you can't walk around.

Put on the headphones, then the Cardboard, and look straight ahead.

  1. 0:00The problem. A video you can only watch from outside.
  2. 0:10The idea. Rewind puts you inside it. Two modes.
  3. 0:28The honest line. Generated, not recorded. Look, don't walk.
  4. 0:36The handover. Headphones first, then the Cardboard. Stay quiet during the video.

Ideas to flesh it out

Make Rewind the visual motif

  • A tape-rewind whoosh under the cube build and again under the logo.
  • The rewind icon (two left-pointing triangles) as the logo mark.
  • The logo letters assembling right to left, like tape winding back.

This ties the name to the pictures without adding any shots.

Building the cubes

  • Recommended: do it in the edit. Render two 360 clips from the same spot: the black sphere with the video at its final size, and the finished room. Reveal the room over the black one with a cell mask that grows outward from the frame, nearest cells first, each cell popping in over about 0.3 seconds, whole build about 3.5 seconds.
  • Alternative: reveal the splat cell by cell with a shader in the app. This gives real 3D cubes, but it needs engineering and I haven't checked that the viewer library supports it.
  • Tint the new cubes slightly blue so the generated part reads as generated. Cells look stretched near the top and bottom of a 360 frame, so keep them small there.

Transition into the concert

  • Recommended: a spotlight iris. A circle of light opens from the center of the black title card and reveals the stadium, with the crowd audio hitting on the first beat.
  • Cube collapse into a white camera flash, then cut. Repeats the cube idea in reverse.
  • Rewind scrub. The party video runs backward fast with a tape whoosh into white, then cuts to the stadium. Uses the motif a second time.
  • Match cut. The video frame shrinks to a point of light that grows into the stage screen. Needs precise alignment.

Seat pins and the countdown

  • Place the pins where each fan stood, so they look anchored in the venue.
  • Label each seat in plain words (Center, Front, Upper right) under the pin.
  • A video can't keep text in front of the viewer's face. Put the countdown low in front at the start of each seat, and repeat it at two more spots 120° apart so it is visible whichever way they look.
  • A small top-down seat map near the bottom of the view with a "you are here" dot would make the pins easier to read.

Honesty and credits

  • Small captions backing up the voice: "Original video preserved. Room generated." in panel 4 and "Venue recreated from fan clips" at the start of panel 6.
  • Credit the fan clips and the party video in one small line on the end card, or in the submission text if it doesn't fit.
  • The Athens clips and the party video need permission if the demo goes public.

Playing it in the Cardboard

  • Render as a 360 video at 3840 x 1920 and 30 fps, and play it in a phone 360 player.
  • Put titles and the first view straight ahead. Ask the judge to look forward and tap to recenter before pressing play.
  • A mono 360 video has no depth. That is fine here because nothing moves toward the viewer.
  • Test on a real phone in the Cardboard before the event: text size, brightness of the title card, and head-turn comfort.
